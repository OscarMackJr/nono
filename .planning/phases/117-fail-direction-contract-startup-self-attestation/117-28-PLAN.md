<objective>
Implement D-37 (LOCKED, `117-CONTEXT.md`): **an ancestor walk that stops at the first non-owned
ancestor is CONTRACT-EXEMPT, not a downgrade.** This closes WR-12, the iteration-5 finding that two
gap-closure plans in the SAME round landed opposite rules for the byte-for-byte identical physical
condition — Plan 117-22 (daemon mirror) argued a skipped ancestor grant is under-*granting*, never
under-*confining*, so it carries no operator-facing signal; Plan 117-23 (CLI mirror,
`dacl_guard.rs`) made the identical condition report `PartiallyApplied` -> `ProceedDowngraded` ->
the non-silenceable D-27 banner on every session whose workspace sits one level under a non-owned
ancestor. **117-22's reading wins** — it is what the SPEC's own standing rule already states
("Contract-exempt skips are NOT downgrades") and what RF-06 exists to protect against (a
permanently-on warning is functionally identical to no warning).

Do **not** revert 117-23: its `walked: bool` field is correct and load-bearing (it is what makes
`application()` genuinely reachable in a coverage-gap state at all). What is wrong is the two-way
collapse — a walk that ran, granted nothing, and stopped at a non-owned ancestor is conflated with a
walk that ran, granted nothing, and did NOT stop that way. D-37 requires three required
consequences, all in this plan: (1) the CLI guards distinguish the two states; (2) the daemon mirror
keeps its two-state shape (no `ProceedDowngraded` reintroduction — no change needed there, verified
in Task 2); (3) the cross-mirror test compares the *classification rule*, not variant name sets.

Also closes WR-14: the brand-new cross-mirror test `every_daemon_variant_is_in_the_cli_variant_set_or
_a_documented_divergence` (landed by the same plan, 117-22, that produced WR-06's fix) reintroduces
the exact first-textual-occurrence parse fragility WR-11 was raised to close on its sibling test —
it does not route through the `DAEMON-DECISION-ENUM` anchor at all.

Purpose: one classification rule, implemented identically by both independent mirrors, provably
by a shared table both are tested against — not two rationales that happen to compile.
Output: a three-arm `application()` on both ancestor guards; reconciled doc comments; a
classification-rule cross-mirror test; a hardened daemon discovery-test anchor.
</objective>
